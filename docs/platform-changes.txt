TEMPLE - THE CHANGE OF DIRECTION
What we have changed, what we are changing now, and what comes next

Plain-text summary. The full argument is in docs/vision.md; the
sequencing is in docs/roadmap.md; the product on one page is in
docs/how-temple-works.md. This document is the change log of the
direction itself.

Last updated: 29 July 2026


===================================================================
1. THE CHANGE IN ONE PARAGRAPH
===================================================================

Temple was gym management software: nine modules that hold a record of
the gym, the same nine every competitor ships, all of them answering
the question "what data does a gym have?" We are rebuilding around a
different question - "what work does an owner do?" - and the answer is
that the owner does nearly all of it while the software watches and
takes notes.

So the product stops being a system of record and becomes a system of
action. Temple does the work: it answers the phone at 2am, chases what
is owed, notices the member who is fading, finds the cover, fills the
6am - and shows its work in a record you can audit and a brief you can
read over coffee. The screens that only exist so a human can operate
the machinery go away. The screens where craft, judgement or evidence
live stay exactly as they are.

The one-line version: we built software that remembers everything and
does nothing; the next Temple does the work and remembers why.


===================================================================
2. WHY WE MOVED
===================================================================

The evidence came from owner interviews and from our own codebase.

From the interviews: an owner dealing with an issue a day from his
platform. One paying GBP 319 a month for Wodify. One paying a
competitor that is also a debt collector, because at least the debt
collector chases the money. One gluing TeamUp, BoxMate and GoHighLevel
together. Members still photographing the whiteboard. And the sharpest
one - a coach entirely happy with his software ("great, can't see any
way of improving it") whose actual business problem was bringing in
members, which no module in any product touches. That is the ceiling
of the model: perfect it, and you have perfectly organised the owner's
unpaid admin.

From our own code: attendance marked by hand per member per session;
every store order waiting for someone to press "mark shipped"; five
separate queues that move only when a human opens them; roughly 35
owner-only settings RPCs, each one a decision forced in advance, out of
context, forever; capability switches for jobs nobody does; fifteen
scheduled jobs of which six are data deletion. And the clincher - we
shipped a quarter of flawless feature catch-up against two owners'
request lists, every line delivered, and the shape of the owner's day
did not change.

The last two months made it explicit. We built world-class visibility
into failed payments: detected the moment Stripe reports them, humane
member notices, at-risk revenue split out of the forecast - and then
the owner gets a list to chase. Cover works the same way: a nightly
sweep emails everyone who could take the class, then emails the owner
that nobody has. Our newest code had learned to nag.

The refutation was already running in production: the AI front desk. It
answers the phone, handles the objection, sends the join link, takes
the payment and books the first class while nobody is awake. That is
not a feature. It is the template for everything else.


===================================================================
3. THE FOUR IDEAS WE NOW BUILD BY
===================================================================

3.1 The unit is the closed loop, not the screen.
    A loop is trigger, decision, action, guardrail, outcome, memory.
    The old product renders state and waits for a human. The new one
    closes loops and shows the receipts.

3.2 The product is an org chart, not a feature list.
    Four jobs, not forty features: the front desk (enquiries and
    joining), the money (failed payments, offers), keeping members
    (churn signals and outreach), and ops (cover, attendance,
    fulfilment). Internally each is an agent with a job description,
    authority and a performance review. On the surface the owner never
    meets an org chart or "hires" anything - they read a job's rules,
    in plain words, and say "sounds right".

3.3 Authority is a dial that moves rightward as trust is earned.
    Every action sits at one of three levels: autonomous,
    approval-gated, or human-reserved. Instead of pre-answering 35
    settings questions out of context, the owner approves concrete
    calls in context; after the third identical approval the card
    offers "always allow this". Policy stops being configured and
    becomes emergent from approvals.

3.4 The interface is the exception handler.
    A screen survives if it holds judgement the software must not
    exercise, trust it has not earned, evidence, or a hand tool for
    physical work (a class roster at six o'clock is a tool, not admin).
    Everything else is a loop that should have closed silently.
    Progress is measured in admin screens deleted.


===================================================================
4. THE NEW SHAPE
===================================================================

Three destinations for the office, one for the floor, and a talk bar.

  Timeline - the staff home. What happened, as short messages from
    Temple in one register, one idea per line. What needs you, as a
    question with exactly two choices and the evidence behind "see the
    details". What got resolved, as one soft line. Scrolling back is
    the record.

  Roster - the people who work for you, and Temple's jobs in plain
    names, each with what it does, this week's number, and how much
    rope it has ("asks first" or "on its own").

  Goals - "200 members by December" as a brief you have given an
    employee, scored from live data and reported on in sentences.

  Today - the coach's landing, deliberately the calendar they already
    know. Same grid, same colours, same controls. What changes is what
    disappears from it, not how it looks.

  The talk bar - the real navigation. Type or speak a sentence and it
    routes to the right loop with its guardrail intact.

Two commitments about this shape. First, the member app does not
change: signing up, buying, booking and tracking already work and are
how the system gets its data. Anything new there is additive - a push
nudge, a milestone message - never a redesign. The redesign is the
staff experience. Second, none of it looks new: every surface is
assembled from the component kit the app already ships, so it reads as
Temple from day one rather than a second product bolted on.

Today's roughly 70 routes do not vanish. They demote to a Back Office
and burn down from there, with two public measures: routes retired,
and owner interventions per member per month.


===================================================================
5. WHAT WE HAVE ALREADY CHANGED
===================================================================

5.1 The AI front desk - the proof, shipped before the vision was
    written. Its own number per gym; it answers calls and texts from
    the gym's live plans and timetable, works objections into a closed
    category set, schedules the chase, emails a one-time join link,
    stages the agreed plan, and books the first class the prospect said
    they would try. The owner teaches it by talking to it and coaches
    it by correcting individual turns, which become standing rules on
    both channels. Self-serve provisioning: the owner presses one
    button and Temple's own Twilio and Vapi accounts do the rest.

5.2 The Timeline, lit up. A single chronological stream per gym,
    read-only to begin with, unioning what already happens: members
    joining, leads captured, payments failing, cover asked and claimed,
    closures, and pending membership change requests. Every line comes
    from one formatter so the register never drifts - one idea, plain
    words, first person only where Temple itself acted, no system
    vocabulary anywhere. Each kind is capability-gated inside the
    query, so a coach and an owner see different streams. Membership
    change requests render as the stream's only cards: one question,
    two choices, decided in place through the existing path.

5.3 The talk bar. A sentence becomes a change. Rule changes, new
    classes, new plans, closures and newsletters are parsed into
    proposals, sanitised client-side against a fixed option table (no
    invented classes, no free plan sold by accident), and rendered as a
    card with exactly two choices. Confirming applies through the same
    writes the manual editors use. Anything it does not handle gets a
    fixed "not from here yet" - it never guesses.

5.4 Settings became sentences. The rule sheet reads the gym's current
    settings back as grouped sentences with tappable values - tap
    "7 days" in "classes can be booked 7 days ahead" and that field's
    options open as chips. Sixteen fields, one table of options shared
    with setup, each saved on its own through the same setters the
    settings cards use.

5.5 Day one is a conversation. A new owner lands in /setup rather than
    a checklist: nine steps in the checklist's own order, each with a
    real container in the chat - the branding picker for the logo, the
    class-types screen's own recurrence editor for the week, the
    Stripe OAuth handoff (which returns the owner to the conversation
    where they left it), a plan builder, health screening by waiver or
    PAR-Q, then team invites, bringing members across and importing
    workout history. Each step takes the fastest input for that step:
    tap where tapping is faster, describe it where describing is. The
    rules step is five one-tap questions with the best practice
    pre-lit. Nothing is written until the owner confirms, and every
    write then runs through the owner's own session on the manual
    editors' exact paths - setup holds no write power of its own.
    Setup is never lost: the Timeline carries the progress header, and
    saying "continue setup" in the bar returns to the right step.

5.6 The money loop acts. The first list to die. Off by default per gym
    and taken on by reading its rules on a Timeline card that only
    appears while a payment is actually failing. An hourly worker works
    each failing subscription as a case through a deterministic policy
    with no model anywhere: the existing notice, then a warm chase
    while Stripe still retries, then - once Stripe gives up - an offer
    of a cheaper plan the gym already sells, or a final note. Anything
    needing judgement becomes a Timeline question with SQL-derived
    evidence; approving executes it, and "always allow this" moves that
    action type to autonomous in the same transaction. Member-facing
    text is filled from owner-approved templates with placeholders
    only, so no model text ever reaches a member, and sends respect
    quiet hours in the gym's own timezone. The hard rules live in SQL:
    one open case per subscription, at most two agent touches, one open
    question at a time, a rejection ends that case's asks for good,
    proposals expire visibly after seven days, and no code path can
    cancel a membership or invent a discount.

5.7 Two more jobs from the same template. Keeping members: a daily
    worker finds regulars who have gone quiet - attended in the last 90
    days, nothing for 21, still on an active plan - and proposes one
    warm note each, capped at three a gym a day, never the same member
    twice inside 45 days, never about health, and writing anyone off
    stays human. Finding cover: an hourly worker re-asks the coaches
    who can claim an unclaimed cover request inside the gym's own
    warning window, once a day at most; it never assigns a coach and
    never moves or cancels a class.

5.8 The Roster and Goals. The Roster shows people beside Temple's jobs
    in plain names, each with a rope pill and, for the owner, dials to
    move a job between "asks first" and "on its own". Goals turns a
    stated target into a brief with a score computed from the live
    roster and never typed in - "143 today, 57 to go, 5 months left".
    The targets schema had existed for months with no screen; in a
    system of record a target is a number to stare at, which is why it
    never got one.

5.9 The programming roadmap. Named training blocks across the year, a
    block strip above the week the coach is writing, and a year view -
    inside the existing editor, from the same component kit. The thing
    the interviews asked for twice. Authoring itself is untouched:
    Temple never rewrites a coach's programming or invents a workout
    unasked.

5.10 A newsletter is a sentence. Say what you want said; the draft
     comes back as the preview card and opens in the existing editor
     with the gym's brand, audience, topics, A/B and unsubscribe
     machinery unchanged. The send button remains the approval -
     nothing sends from the bar. The prompt is forbidden from inventing
     dates, prices or names.

5.11 The ledger. Agent actions are written to their own table with no
     client write path at all, and read into the Timeline. This is the
     substrate for "why did that happen" and for undo.

Alongside all of this, the growth surfaces that shipped just before the
turn are now part of the substrate rather than the point: the website
builder (multi-page, real SEO, live schedule and pricing that cannot go
stale), the storefront, the comms suite, tags and auto-tagging, and the
import machinery for gyms switching from another platform.


===================================================================
6. WHAT WE ARE DOING NOW AND NEXT
===================================================================

Remaining, roughly in order:

  - Attendance stops being transcription. Everyone booked marks as
    attended unless the coach unticks them; no-shows resolve after
    class; the patterns flow to the retention job with nobody typing
    them in. The coach's screen does not change - the work inside it
    disappears.

  - The member in hand. "Show me Marcus" returns the picture rather
    than a profile page - plan, tenure, the thing worth knowing, and an
    offer to act - with the full profile one tap deeper.

  - Class fill, which forces push notifications into the product for
    the first time, attached to a message members actually want
    (a spot in tomorrow's 6am they usually attend).

  - Sequences described rather than built, over the automation engine
    that already exists.

  - Store fulfilment and the imported-member invite chase - two more
    queues that become receipts.

  - Every chart ends in a verb: the analysis verdicts become messages
    with drafted actions, with the full page surviving in the Back
    Office for the deep dive. Aggregates of closed categories only,
    never an individual's health.

  - The scheduled sweeps get their first screen. Every sweep already
    logs what it did; the only interface today is a SQL query.

  - The Manage hub collapses into the three destinations, and the Back
    Office burndown is published and worked.

Also open, and deliberately not designed yet: how agent usage is
metered and billed (Temple is GBP 299 a month plus VAT, all-inclusive,
today), and the go-to-market wedge - noting that the front desk can
answer a gym's phone while that gym still runs its old platform, which
means we can be switched on before we are the system of record we
replace.


===================================================================
7. WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE
===================================================================

  - The member app. Book, Track, Programming, the store, plan changes,
    family accounts, athlete mode: unchanged, because they work and
    because members feeding the system is what makes every loop
    possible.

  - The machinery. Every RPC, worker, cron job and RLS policy survives;
    it stops being something a human operates and becomes the loops'
    sensors and hands. The capability matrix stays the authorisation
    substrate - an agent's authority is a row in the same system that
    decides what a coach may do.

  - Craft and evidence keep their screens. Writing programming, running
    a class, listening back to a call, reviewing an import's judgement
    calls, a coach checking their pay.

  - The trust architecture, by name and unchanged. Waivers and health
    questionnaires are signed by the member's own hand and can never be
    pre-filled by software. No model-authored free text reaches a
    health field - an injury mention sets a flag, nothing more.
    Objections and health markers are closed category sets. One-time
    auth links travel by email only. Changes to an agent's brief arrive
    as drafts and wait for the owner. Writing a member off is a human
    judgement. Prices are the owner's, permanently. Every action is one
    tap from "why?" and one tap from undo, and every memory the jobs
    acquire inherits the existing retention and purge discipline.


===================================================================
8. HOW WE WILL KNOW IT WORKED
===================================================================

  - Owner interventions per member per month, driven down release by
    release. This is the north star.
  - Back Office routes retired, and Back Office visits per owner per
    week. When a screen's visits hit zero for a quarter it is deleted,
    and the deletion is a release note.
  - Recovered revenue, saved members, filled classes and placed cover -
    outcomes in sentences, not funnels.

A screen deleted is progress. A screen rebuilt prettier is not.
